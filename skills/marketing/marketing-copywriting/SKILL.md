## Rule

Copy on the user's sites is written for small and mid-sized businesses and
their local customers. It is true, concrete to the trade and the region, and
short. It is never SaaS marketing: no proof bars, no urgency, no "Start free
trial". The brief is the project's concept doc (`docs/concept.md`): its
facts, sources, audience and open questions. There is no separate marketing
context file.

### 1. Diagnose the input before writing

Say in one line which case it is, and why:

| Case | Input | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Blank** | a brief, an old site, notes | build the argument from the facts; free on angle, never on facts |
| **B. Real voice** | the owner's own text | keep their argument and words; fix the buried lead, vague lines, tells and structure |
| **C. Point edit** | "change this headline" | change only that unit, plus the minimum around it |

In B and C, the author's choices are deliberate: their words, what they
spent words on, what they left out. Don't upgrade casual words to corporate
ones, and don't add sections they skipped. Finally, apply the
**leave-it-alone test**: revert every change that is different but not
measurably better.

### 2. Facts first; nothing invented

- **Sources.** Every claim traces to a source recorded in the concept: the
  old site, the legal notice, the owner, a document they sent.
- **Never invent:** clients, numbers, years in business, reviews, ratings,
  certificates, team members, quotes, opening hours or prices.
- **Gaps** become open questions in the concept, never page text. The site's
  page check fails while placeholder text is left. In a draft shown to the
  user, flag a gap inline as `[NEED: proof that …]`, and state what evidence
  would unlock the claim.
- **Claims match reality.** No "24/7" without a rota, and nothing the page
  itself refutes. Say who it is *not* for, where that helps.
- **De-slopping** never means swapping a vague word for an invented number.
- **No names without approval.** Clients and projects aren't named unless
  approved; describe past work generically ("a carpentry business in the
  region").

### 3. Voice

- **German sites use *Sie*,** with an identifiable *wir*. Each language is
  written in its own right by a person, never machine-translated. The
  agent's German is a draft until the owner or a native writer has read it.
- **Plain and concrete.** "Anfragen von der Website landen direkt im
  Postfach" beats "Optimierung Ihrer Kommunikationsprozesse". Short
  sentences, active voice, no noun chains, no exclamation marks, no
  rhetorical-question headlines.
- **Specific to the trade and region.** Use the business's own terms; an old
  site's trade word may stay in the copy even when the URL uses the search
  term.
- **Fixed terms.** One word per concept per project ("Website", not
  alternating with "Webseite"), recorded in the concept.
- **Ration the punch.** Most lines are plain statements; a page gets one or
  two striking moments, usually the headline.
- **Clarity beats cleverness.** Anglicisms only where they are the normal
  word (Website, CRM), never "Solutions", "Insights", "Journey".

### 4. Structure that worked

- **Hero.** A short, slogan-led headline, one sentence of lead, the primary
  action ("Anfrage senden") plus phone as the secondary. Long hero text was
  rejected.
- **One argument per section,** in an order that walks the visitor's
  situation: offer → their situation → process → terms → who you are →
  contact. A hero followed by a list of features and a CTA is a list, not an
  argument.
- **Service pages** explain each service plainly: what it is, how it goes,
  what the customer gets, the next step. Name each page the way customers
  search.
- **References** only with real photos and approved names.
- **Contact page:** phone, email, address, the region served, and the form,
  which says beside the button what happens to the data.
- **Privacy policy** opens with one plain sentence ("Kurz gesagt: …").
- **404:** one line in the business's own terms, plus links to the main
  pages.
- **CTAs** name what the visitor gets or does ("Termin vereinbaren",
  "Angebot anfragen"), never "Click here", "Learn more" or "Submit". Use one
  wording per intent across the whole site.

### 5. Test every line

1. **Picture it:** can the reader see it happen?
2. **Prove it:** could it be shown true or false?
3. **Swap test:** would a competitor print it unchanged?

Three passes: keep it. Two: sharpen the weak point. One or none: rewrite
from the facts, or flag the gap. A sentence that claims something must carry
a figure, a name, how it works, or a concrete step.

### 6. Remove AI tells

**Never:**

- **Contrast reveal:** "Es geht nicht um X, sondern um Y", "It's not X,
  it's Y", "nicht nur X, sondern auch Y". State Y directly with its reason.
- **Negation lists:** "Keine X, keine Y, keine Z". Say what happens; keep at
  most one real absence next to the CTA.
- **Trailing pile-ons:** a finished claim followed by a comma and more
  benefits.
- **Self-answered questions** ("Das Ergebnis? …") and **colon reveals**
  ("Das Beste: …").
- **Stock openers and closers:** "In der heutigen schnelllebigen Welt",
  "Egal ob Sie … oder …", "Stellen Sie sich vor", "Am Ende des Tages", a
  quotable tidy last line.
- **Weasel sources** ("Experten sind sich einig"), importance puffery, fake
  vulnerability.
- **Clichés, German:** maßgeschneiderte Lösungen · ganzheitlicher Ansatz ·
  innovative Technologien · zukunftssicher · Digitalisierung vorantreiben ·
  Ihr Partner für · Wir begleiten Sie · auf Augenhöhe · Mehrwert schaffen ·
  Synergien · Wettbewerbsvorsprung.
- **Clichés, English:** unlock · seamless · cutting-edge · end-to-end (as a
  claim) · transform your business · streamline · empower · supercharge ·
  world-class · next-generation · game-changer · leverage · delve.

**At most once per section:**

- fragment drumbeats ("Schnell. Sauber. Fertig.")
- reflexive triples
- uniform sentence rhythm
- one thing called by several names
- abstract subjects ("die Plattform ermöglicht…") instead of the customer
- empty intensifiers ("wirklich", "einfach", "genuinely")

Replace a flagged word with the fact it stood in for, never with a synonym.

**Dashes.** In German the spaced en dash is normal punctuation
(*Gedankenstrich*, and in ranges); use it sparingly and correctly. In
English short copy (headlines, CTAs, hero), use no em dashes.

### 7. German typography

- Quotes „…", dates `04.10.2026`, amounts `1.500 €` with a no-break space.
- `tel:` links in international form.
- A non-breaking hyphen in compounds that must not break (`E‑Mail`), and a
  soft hyphen in very long heading words.
- `lang` attributes on foreign words, so hyphenation and screen readers get
  them right.
- Alt texts and `aria-label`s are copy too: in German, describing what the
  photo shows.
- Language-specific line-end rules on other-language sites (e.g. no
  single-letter words at line ends in Polish).

### 8. Self-check before delivering

1. Search headlines, subheads and short copy for "nicht", "kein", "ohne",
   "?", ":" and dashes, and check each hit against section 6.
2. Inspect every sentence over 20 words; three or more commas usually means
   a pile-on.
3. Count fragments, triples and capped words per section.
4. Read it aloud. Mark anything that sounds like a press release.
5. Run the swap test on the headline, subhead and every section heading.
6. Check that every number, name and claim is in the concept's sources.

### 9. Deliver

- **Point edit (C):** only the changed lines, each with a one-line reason.
- **Page copy (A/B):**
  1. the copy by section (headline, lead, CTA; each section's heading and
     body)
  2. short notes on the key choices
  3. 2–3 alternatives for the headline and the main CTA
  4. the page title and meta description (see
     [marketing-seo-audit](../marketing-seo-audit/SKILL.md))
  5. the open questions for the owner
- **Copy goes into content modules** (`src/lib/<topic>.ts`), never into
  components.

## Why

The user kept the upstream copywriting skill's honesty rules and its
AI-tell list, and both are the core of this one. Its SaaS templates clashed
with every site: proof bars of logos and "10,000+ teams", testimonials that
didn't exist, urgency, and button formulas like "Start free trial". The
rules on German voice, the cliché lists, typography and "nothing invented"
come from the user's own sites and concepts, where invented facts and
generic agency phrases were the two things most often cut.

## Exceptions

- **The owner's own wording wins** over these rules once they have approved
  it (case B), unless it is untrue.
- **Long-form articles** (rare on these sites) may use more transitions and
  a question-led structure. The tells list still applies.
- **Legal pages** follow legal wording, not marketing voice.
